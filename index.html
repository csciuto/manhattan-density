Census tract densities in Manhattan, 2010 and 2020

An update to Shlomo Angel and Patrick Lamson-Hall's 1910/2010 density plate, using its density bands in persons per hectare. Flip between years and the island barely twitches. 270 of 310 tracts stay in the same band. The towers of 1910 don't come back anywhere.

Drag to turn the island · hover a tract for numbers
2020

Data. NYC Dept. of City Planning, 2010–2020 Census core geographies workbook (from U.S. Census Bureau 2010 and 2020 redistricting and DHC files), total population (Pop1) by 2020 tract, divided by the workbook's land acres (converted to hectares). DCP publishes 2010 counts already re-tabulated to 2020 tracts, so both years share the same 310 shapes. Angel and Lamson-Hall divide by the area of built city blocks, without streets or parks, so their figures run about 30% higher than these.

Shapes. DCP 2020 census tracts (Bytes of the Big Apple, clipped to shoreline), simplified for drawing. Heights use one linear scale in both years, sized so the original chart's 2,400 p/ha ceiling would still fit.

Change view. Height is 2020 density. Color is the change in persons per hectare from 2010.

After. Visual approach, density bands and 1910 figures from Shlomo Angel and Patrick Lamson-Hall, The Rise and Fall of Manhattan's Densities, 1800–2010, Marron Institute of Urban Management Working Paper 18, New York University, 2014 (PDF). Their maps draw on census data from the National Historical Geographic Information System.
